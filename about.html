The Chronicle

A marriage of
microbiology
& wood smoke.

At Crust & Crumb, we believe baking is not a trade of secrets, but a discipline of patient observation. We work exclusively with heritage grains, natural leavening, and wild heat.

Our journey began with a specimen tube, a temperature-controlled chamber, and a fragment of a wild yeast culture kept alive since 1924 in the mountains of the Pacific Northwest. While industrial bakeries rely on isolated commercial strains to force rapid rise, our focus is the complex ecology of wild lactobacilli.

We set out to master the precise chemistry of fermentation. By monitoring hydration, ambient barometric pressure, and regional flour blends, we developed a system that respects the character of raw, stone-milled wheat.

Today, we maintain that same rigorous attention to detail. Every loaf that slides off the wooden peel and onto our basalt hearth represents thirty-six hours of careful metabolic development, resulting in a dark, blistered crust and an open, custard-like interior.

Origin

The Centenarian Culture

Our starter is a living lineage. Originally captured in rural Idaho during the early twentieth century, this specific culture thrives on regional microbes. By feeding it twice daily with a custom ratio of organic rye and hard white spring wheat, we maintain a delicate acidity balance that yields our signature complex flavor without harsh sourness.

It requires constant care. Weather, temperature, and atmospheric pressure influence its activity every single day.
Infrastructure

The Firewood Hearth

We cook directly on solid stone. Our custom-engineered, indirect-fired oven is heated exclusively with sustainably harvested local hardwoods. This design holds incredible thermal mass, radiating deep, dry heat that caramelizes the grain sugars rapidly, locking in the inner crumb moisture.

Steam is injected manually during the first eight minutes to allow the expansion of the loaf envelope.
